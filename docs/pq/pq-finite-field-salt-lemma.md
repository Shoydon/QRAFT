# Finite uniform salt support: direct oracle hiding bound

2026-10-30. Elementary probability proof and scope assessment; no code or deployment changes. This establishes a leaf-hiding ingredient. It does not establish an entire Fiat–Shamir compiler theorem or postquantum custody claim.

The primary comparison is Sharp–Grubbs, [GordieHowe](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/2615.pdf), DefinitionI.1 and its following derivation (printed pp130–131), TheoremI.5 step3 (p134), and especially LemmaI.7 (p135). The local full PDF and its SHA256 are recorded in `pq-gordiehowe-instantiation-review.md`. The leaf estimate is not TheoremI.3: that theorem concerns completeness.

## Statement

Let S be a finite salt set of cardinality M, and A a finite set of possible leaf contents of cardinality N. Let enc:A×S→Q_leaf be injective. Let H be a uniformly sampled random function on its finite domain, with output length at least b bits; write h for the first b output bits and R=2^b. Other domain parts and the remaining output bits can be retained in the experiment.

For fixed H and m define the distribution

`P_(H,m)(z) = |{s∈S : h(enc(m,s))=z}| / M`.

Let U_R be uniform on the R possible digests, and set

`δ(H)=max_(m∈A) TV(P_(H,m), U_R)`.

Then

`E_H δ(H) ≤ ε_leaf(M,N,R)`

where

`ε_leaf = min(1, ½√((R−1)/M) + √(ln N/(2M)))`.

For N=1 the second term is zero. No property of S beyond its cardinality, uniform sampling and injective encoding is required. In particular, S need not be all bit strings of any length.

## Proof

Fix m. The M digests `Y_s=h(enc(m,s))` are independent uniform samples in a set of size R. For each z,

`E P(z)=1/R`, and `Var P(z)=(1/R)(1−1/R)/M`.

Therefore

`E χ²(P||U_R) = E [R Σ_z(P(z)−1/R)²] = (R−1)/M`.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives `TV(P,U_R)≤½√χ²(P||U_R)`; Jensen then yields

`E TV(P,U_R)≤a := ½√((R−1)/M)`.

Changing one sample Y_s changes the empirical distribution by total variation at most1/M. Its distance to U_R consequently also changes by at most1/M. The bounded-differences exponential estimate is

`E exp(λ(f_m−E f_m)) ≤ exp(λ²/(8M))`, where `f_m=TV(P_(H,m),U_R)`.

Independence between different messages is not needed in the following step. For λ>0, exponential domination and Jensen imply

`E max_m f_m ≤ a + (ln N)/λ + λ/(8M)`.

Optimizing λ gives the stated `a+√(ln N/(2M))` bound. Equivalently, a useful tail estimate is

`Pr_H[δ(H)>a+t] ≤ min(1,N exp(−2Mt²))`.

The proof depends only on the M independent oracle samples for each fixed m. Arbitrary injective salt encodings and finite uniform supports therefore give exactly the same result, with M replacing the number of byte-string salts. There is no hybrid comparing serialized field salts to uniformly random bytes.

## Joint salt/digest replacement, including opened salts

A digest-only replacement is insufficient if its original salt is subsequently disclosed. The correct hybrid, used by GordieHowe LemmaI.7, replaces the joint pair.

At fixed H,m, the real pair is `(S,h(enc(m,S)))` with S uniform. The ideal pair is generated by drawing D uniform among R digests and then drawing S uniformly from the preimage of D. If the preimage is empty, choose an arbitrary fixed distribution on S. Then

`TV(real_pair, ideal_pair) = TV(P_(H,m), U_R)`.

To check this, let n_z be the number of preimages of z. For n_z>0, summing the absolute joint-probability differences over that fiber gives `|n_z/M−1/R|`. For n_z=0 the real pair has zero mass and the ideal pair has total mass1/R. Summing over all z and dividing by two proves equality. Empty fibers require no additional error term.

Conditional preimage sampling is an analytical hybrid. Exhaustive sampling is permitted for this statistical comparison; it is not an efficiency claim about the deployed prover or actual ZK simulator. Proving the efficiency of a full protocol simulator remains a separate compiler obligation.

## Whole Merkle executions and quantum side information

Fix H. Before a commitment message, condition on its preceding execution and on the honest source prover's coins. The contents of that message's leaves are fixed, and their salts must be fresh independent uniform elements of S. Replace their joint salt/digest pairs one at a time as above. Subsequent computation, including roots, future challenges, adaptive choice of opened positions, and disclosure of opened salts, is the same channel applied to these pairs. A hybrid over at most npos leaves bounds the whole-view change by

`λ(H)=npos δ(H)`; hence `E_H λ(H)≤npos ε_leaf`.

For several salt-support sizes, sum the respective bounds, or use the smallest support size as a conservative common bound. The leaf alphabet must include every possible canonical leaf-content encoding; if an indexed domain is used, include its index among the finite contents or account for it in the maximum separately. Joint injectivity and fixed lengths/tags are essential.

This comparison also permits a quantum auxiliary state correlated with H and with the history. At fixed H and classical m/history, tensor the same auxiliary density operator with the two classical pair distributions. Their trace distance is the classical TV above. Controlled channels cannot increase trace distance. Averaging over the classical oracle and history therefore gives the same bound, using the maximum over m even when the adversary selects m from arbitrary information about H. Revealing the entire classical oracle is a stronger side-information experiment than permitting only bounded quantum oracle access. Conditioning on independent output tails does not alter the occupancy calculation, and the maximum over m already covers content selection based on those tails.

The indispensable condition is freshness: prior side information must not already contain a chosen salt correlated with the leaf content before this replacement. Subsequent salt-dependent side information is allowed as the common continuation channel. Independent supports or prefix-free encodings must prevent one salt value from being interpreted as another leaf's content; shared raw oracle inputs from an invalid encoding defeat the hypothesis. If the actual oracle has already been programmed at relevant leaf points, the lemma cannot simply be reapplied as if those values were uniform. GordieHowe's simulator programs stream points, a separate domain, which preserves the needed leaf-oracle distribution; another compiler must establish its own corresponding fact.

This proves the leaf-replacement bound used in GordieHowe's argument under finite uniform support. It does not by itself prove that another transcript adapter satisfies its TheoremI.5 or I.17, or that a different compiler's simulator has the same programming/freshness structure.

## Sixteen Goldilocks salts

Take `p=18446744069414584321`, `S=Fp^16`, `M=p^16`, and a512-bit digest (`R=2^512`). Canonical fixed-width serialization of each field tuple is injective. Under ideal independent uniform field sampling:

- `log2 M ≈1023.9999999946256`.
- The occupancy term is approximately `2^−256.9999999973128`.
- If leaf contents have at most22000 bytes, then `N≤Σ_(j=0)^22000 256^j <2^(176001)`, and the concentration term is smaller than about `2^−504`.
- With `npos≤2^20`, the dominant whole-leaf hybrid error is about `2^−237` per proof. These are **leaf-hiding terms**, not complete proof-security bits.

The support's exclusion of noncanonical64-bit words causes no2^−28 loss: the proof directly analyzes the uniform distribution on Fp^16. For a joint leaf containing several independently salted matrix rows, the full support is a product; using only M=p^16 is conservative when at least one independent sixteen-field tuple is present. The precise native grouping and per-leaf injectivity still need checking.

This corrects the overly restrictive suggestion in the earlier instantiation review that matching128 uniform bytes is necessary. Uniform bytes are one simple route; direct finite-support analysis is another rigorous route for this leaf estimate. It changes the proof's salt-cardinality parameter, not the hash primitive.

## From ideal salts to implemented randomness

A deterministic seeded generator does not generate a statistically uniform distribution on Fp^16: its support is limited by its seed space. It needs a computational hybrid, not the preceding statistical claim applied directly to its outputs. For QPT adversaries, add the appropriate quantum-PRG distinguishing advantages for all private streams, with independent seeds, correct rejection sampling, domain separation and the actual public/auxiliary transcript included in the reduction. State the PRG assumption explicitly.

Consequently the implementation can at most inherit a **conditional computational QPT ZK** claim after the correct compiler/HVZK mapping and PRG hybrid are established. The ideal-salt statistical calculation does not establish everlasting ZK for a finite-seed implementation against unlimited later computation. Nor does this lemma repair the v3 indexed-VC/compiler mismatch or its prior-proof custody boundary.
